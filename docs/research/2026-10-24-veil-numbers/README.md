# Why Veil: the numbers

> Dated research evidence, not a guarantee about this app. Review [current data protection](../../SECURITY.md) before reusing product claims, and re-check dated external figures before publication.

Checked on 24 October 2026. Every figure below was read on its source page by us; the full evidence (exact quotes, samples, caveats and confidence) is in [work/](work/). Prices and surveys from companies that sell security products are marked as such.

**The case in one paragraph.** People already paste their own documents into ChatGPT and Claude, mostly at work and mostly through tools their employer never approved. UK regulators now say in writing that client data must not go into public AI tools without safeguards, and breaches that involve unapproved AI cost more than average. Firms do not want bans; they want a safe way to keep using AI. Veil hides personal values before a document leaves the laptop, puts them back in the answer, and learns each firm's own identifiers from corrections through Mubit.

## The rules now say it out loud

- **Solicitors.** The SRA warns about client information entered into public AI tools and expects contractual, technical and organisational safeguards before client information goes into any AI system. It quotes the Upper Tribunal ([2026] UKUT 81 (IAC)) that putting client letters into ChatGPT places them in the public domain and may lose privilege. [SRA, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/misuse-ai/)
- **The Law Society** tells solicitors not to put confidential data into free online AI tools. [Generative AI: the essentials](https://www.lawsociety.org.uk/topics/ai-and-lawtech/generative-ai-the-essentials)
- **Accountants.** ICAEW's tax guidance (January 2026) says putting client data into public AI tools is likely to breach confidentiality unless the client consents, and that inputs should not identify the client. [ICAEW PCRT guidance](https://www.icaew.com/technical/tax/working-in-tax/pcrt/topical-guidance-application-of-pcrt-to-ethical-use-of-artificial-intelligence-tools)
- **Surveyors and RICS-regulated agents.** Since 9 March 2026 they must not upload a client's confidential data to AI without express written consent. [RICS standard](https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/conduct-competence/responsible-use-of-ai/client-information-note)
- **Financial firms.** The FCA adds no AI rules: firms can outsource AI but stay responsible for it. [FCA, Oct 2026](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-poor-practice/ai-financial-services)
- **The ICO** warns that a simple ban drives staff to use AI under the radar ([Stephen Almond, March 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/staff-use-ai-blanket-ban-ever-answer-9yoce)). Bans are falling: Cisco found outright GenAI bans dropped from 28% to 7% of organisations ([Cisco 2026 Data and Privacy Benchmark](https://www.cisco.com/c/dam/en_us/about/doing_business/trust-center/docs/cisco-privacy-benchmark-study-2026.pdf)).

**The legal argument, stated carefully.** Pseudonymised data stays personal data for whoever holds the key; shared without the key it "may be anonymous information" in the recipient's hands ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/pseudonymisation); the EU Court of Justice said something similar in EDPS v SRB, [4 Sept 2025](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2025-09/cp250107en.pdf)). Veil keeps the key on the user's side, so the provider receives far less. The firm still has full UK GDPR duties, leftover context can still identify people, and we never say Veil makes anyone "compliant" or that its output is "anonymised".

## Why now: what happened to chats that people thought were private

- **May 2025 to January 2026.** A US court ordered OpenAI to keep ChatGPT logs, including deleted chats (the duty ended on 26 September 2025), and later to hand 20 million de-identified logs to news plaintiffs. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/response-to-nyt-data-demands/), [court order, 5 Jan 2026](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.612697/gov.uscourts.nysd.612697.1087.0.pdf)
- **July 2025.** Nearly 4,500 shared ChatGPT conversations, some with names and health details, turned up in Google; OpenAI removed the feature. [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91376687/google-indexing-chatgpt-conversations)
- **August 2025.** Anthropic began training on consumer chats unless users opt out, keeping them for five years if training is on. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/updates-to-our-consumer-terms)
- **January 2026.** A third-party AI chat app exposed 300 million messages from 25 million users. [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/massive-ai-chat-app-leaked-millions-of-users-private-conversations/)
- **February 2025, UK.** Law firm Hill Dickinson logged more than 32,000 ChatGPT hits in seven days, then restricted access. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cglyjn7le2ko)

## Competition, honestly

Hiding values and putting them back in the answer is no longer rare: at least ten tools do it, from browser extensions for individuals (Quieta, Cloak, PII Shield) to legal products in Europe (anymize, Auryth, ndaact.ai, Mino, LexPrivacy) and enterprise APIs (Limina, formerly Private AI). OpenAI released a free PII-masking model in April 2026, so basic detection is no longer a moat. Full list with links: [work/market-and-deals.md](work/market-and-deals.md#5-direct-competitors-redact-or-pseudonymise-before-the-llm).

What Veil can claim:
1. **It learns each firm's own identifiers.** Every correction becomes a training example for the next adapter version through Mubit. Only two competitors show anything similar, both narrower (one browser, or one matter inside their own app). OpenAI reports that fine-tuning its filter on a domain lifted F1 from 54% to 96%, which is the effect per-firm learning targets.
2. **True redaction of the file itself**, not only a pseudonymised copy of the text.
3. **UK identifiers** (sort codes, NI numbers, UK postcodes, NHS numbers) with checksum validation.
4. **Works with the user's own ChatGPT or Claude** rather than replacing them.

## Why Mubit fits: people keep correcting the same things

From the team's earlier research (sources linked, not re-checked in this pass): about 30% of real chats contain explicit user feedback, most of it corrections ([Don-Yehiya et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10944)); users report models drifting back to old habits after a few dozen messages despite memory and custom instructions ([OpenAI community forum](https://community.openai.com/t/custom-instructions-and-memory-preferences-repeatedly-drift-in-long-chatgpt-conversations/1406547)). A redaction miss is the same kind of repeated correction, and Mubit turns it into a weight update instead of a reminder.

## Use with care

- Most prompt-level leakage rates come from security vendors (Cyberhaven 39.7%, Netskope, Harmonic, LayerX). They are useful but marked as vendor data in [work/ai-data-leakage.md](work/ai-data-leakage.md).
- We found no ICO fine or statement that pasting personal data into ChatGPT is, by itself, a breach. Do not claim one.
- No primary survey of UK letting agents or accountants pasting client data into AI was found; the accountancy surveys are weak (global or small samples).
- Our own measurement: pattern rules alone hid 38.5% of personal values in our test on 1,459 synthetic documents; the trained model's figures are pending.
